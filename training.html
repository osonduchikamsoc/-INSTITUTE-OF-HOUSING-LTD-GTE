OUR PURPOSE

Our Mission: Driving Excellence in Housing

The Nigerian housing and real estate sectors face persistent challenges from unregulated actors. The Institute is taking a decisive stand to reform the industry by promoting professionalism, accountability and excellence.

02
OUR OBJECTIVES

Our Core Objectives

  • Remove Institutional Bottlenecks: Work with active stakeholders to identify and remove institutional bottlenecks within the sector.
  • Prevent Fraud: Protect the public by promoting qualified and competent professionals within the housing and real estate industry.
  • Safeguard Investments: Restore sanity within the industry and promote professional growth through legal, technical and administrative training.
  • Promote Professionalism: Admit qualified housing practitioners and help eliminate unqualified practitioners from the industry, thereby reducing disputes and the risk of litigation.

OUR LEADERSHIP

President Institute of Housing Administration & Management MEET OUR PRESIDENT

Trained by Leaders, Built for the Real Estate Industry

Every training program is only as strong as the people behind it, and ours is led by Ofili Martins, ACA (Ireland), CNA, FCCFR, FCNA — President of the Institute of Housing Administration and Management Ltd/GTE. His professional accounting and regulatory credentials reflect years of rigorous training and continued standing within recognized accounting and financial oversight bodies — the same discipline he now brings into how our trainees are assessed, mentored, and prepared for the field.

Before leading the Institute, he served as Director, Military Agencies Audit Department, within the Office of the Auditor-General for the Federation — a public-sector role centered on financial accountability, compliance, and institutional oversight at a national level. That experience sits at the core of our training philosophy: property management is not just about showing houses or collecting rent, it is a profession that requires the same standards of accountability, documentation, and ethics found in any serious financial discipline.

The Institute was founded to respond directly to a gap in Nigeria's housing sector — a market where unregulated agents and informal practices have too often led to financial loss, disputes, and costly litigation for landlords, tenants, and investors alike. Rather than treat this as an unavoidable cost of doing business, our President set out to build an institution that trains university graduates into accountable, qualified property management professionals, replacing guesswork with process and informal hustle with verifiable competence.

That mission shapes everything you will experience as a trainee here. Our curriculum is built around the practical realities of the Nigerian property market — rent administration, sales documentation, client management, and dispute avoidance — while holding every trainee to a standard of professionalism comparable to what is obtainable in more established housing markets overseas. You are not being trained to simply "get by" in the industry; you are being trained to raise the bar for it.

We also measure ourselves against a global benchmark. Much like the Chartered Institute of Housing in London — a professional body with over forty-five thousand members shaping housing policy internationally — our President's long-term vision is for this Institute to become a nationally and eventually globally recognized authority on housing administration. As a trainee, that means the certificate you earn here is designed to mean something: a signal to employers, clients, and colleagues that you were trained to a professional, accountable standard.

If you are serious about building a real career in property and real estate management — not just picking up informal tips, but learning the profession the right way — you are in the right place.
